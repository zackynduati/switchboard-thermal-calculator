Switchboard Thermal Calculator

Estimates the internal air temperature of a low-voltage switchboard from its heat losses and enclosure size, then sizes the filter fans needed to keep it within your limit. Loaded with the Phase II MDB as a worked example.

Enclosure

Body size without the plinth. Surface factors follow IEC 60890.

Site and limits

Breakers are usually rated for 40 °C air around them and need derating above that. Check the derating tables of your devices before raising the inside limit.

Heat losses

Default watt values are typical figures. Replace them with manufacturer data.

Breakers and switching devices

Loss scales with the square of load: loss = quantity × loss at rated current × (load ÷ rated)².

Busbars and cables inside the board

Three loaded phases, neutral unloaded. Section is per conductor, so a 100 × 10 bar is 1000 mm². The AC factor covers skin and proximity effect on large bars.

Limits of this method

  • It gives the average air temperature inside one enclosure. Air at the top of a tall board runs hotter than the average.
  • Partitioned boards (Form 3 or 4) trap heat in each cubicle. Check the most heavily loaded cubicle as its own board, with its sides set to adjoining cubicle.
  • IEC 61439-1 accepts temperature-rise verification by calculation only up to 1600 A. Above that, the design must be verified by test or by comparison with a tested design, so treat this as a design estimate.
  • Fans cool towards room temperature, never below it. Fan cut-outs also lower the IP rating to that of the filter.
